\section{Introduction}
The pursuit of creating intelligent and autonomous agents that can seamlessly operate in real-world settings and complete complex tasks has been a foundational goal in artificial intelligence~\citep{wooldridge1995intelligent,Minsky88,bubeck2023sparks}. The advancement of LLMs~\citep{gpt4,gpt4o,deepseek_v3,qwen2,songoptimization,sun2025maskpro,shi-etal-2025-personax} has opened new avenues in this domain. Despite their impressive capabilities, single-agent LLMs often struggle to manage the inherent complexity in many sophisticated tasks, necessitating the use of MAS \citep{feng2025one,camel}. By leveraging collaborative interactions among multiple LLM agents, MAS can effectively tackle diverse tasks, such as mathematical reasoning \citep{agentverse}, software development \citep{chatdev}, and scientific discovery \citep{agent_laboratory,ai_scientist}, moving us closer to realizing artificial general intelligence capable of generalizing across various domains \citep{goertzel2007artificial,clune2020aigas,jin2025rankflow,jin2025apeer,ni2024harnessing}.

Recently, TTS has emerged as an effective approach to enhance LLM performance, particularly for complex mathematical reasoning tasks \citep{r1,snell2025scaling,tts,s1,rebase,jin2024impact,wang2025larger,li2024graph,nie2024text,nie2024code,yuan2025superficial,wang2024large}. Techniques such as Monte Carlo Tree Search \citep{mcts,zhang2024o1codero1replicationcoding,llama_berry}, large-scale reinforcement learning \citep{r1,o1,o1rl}, and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on detailed reasoning chains \citep{s1,limo}, have been extensively utilized to facilitate TTS and improve chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. However, TTS for collaborative reasoning within multi-agent systems, where multiple agents with diverse expertise collaborate on complex problems, remains an important open problem. Thus, this work investigates how to effectively scale multi-agent collaboration and reasoning to enhance performance across a wide array of complicated tasks.

We demonstrate that the collaborative reasoning capabilities of LLMs can be effectively enhanced through SFT on a rich dataset comprising hundreds of multi-agent collaborative reasoning traces. Leveraging the fine-tuned LLMs within MAS allows adaptive scaling of agent collaboration, significantly improving performance in complex tasks, including general understanding, mathematical reasoning, and coding. Specifically, \ding{182} we first construct a high-quality multi-agent collaborative reasoning dataset by solving diverse and challenging problems using an open-source MAS. To ensure dataset quality and support long CoT, we filter low-quality examples and utilize DeepSeek-R1 \citep{r1} to generate robust reasoning traces. Subsequently, we SFT an LLM on our curated dataset \textbf{M500}, which contains 500 detailed multi-agent collaborative reasoning traces. The resulting model, termed \textbf{M1-32B}, is designed to proficiently collaborate and scale reasoning from a multi-expert perspective. \ding{183} To further optimize adaptive scaling in the MAS, we introduce a "\textbf{CEO}" agent powered by M1-32B, inspired by the observation that leaderless groups in human societies often lack effectiveness and coherent direction \citep{cruz1999impact,hackman2002leading}. This agent dynamically guides discussions, effectively managing collaborative efforts and reasoning depth to enhance the overall performance of the system.

We conduct extensive experiments to validate our approach by fine-tuning Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct \citep{qwen25} on our dataset M500, obtaining the model M1-32B, and integrating it within the AgentVerse \citep{agentverse} multi-agent framework. Testing across various task categories—including general understanding, mathematical reasoning, and coding—demonstrates that our M1-32B significantly outperforms the baseline Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct within the MAS. For example, our method achieves a 12\% improvement on GPQA-Diamond \citep{gpqa}, 41\% improvement on AIME2024 \citep{aime2024}, and 10\% improvement on MBPP-Sanitized \citep{mbpp}, achieving a comparable performance to DeepSeek-R1 on MATH-500 and MBPP-Sanitized.

In summary, our contributions are: \ding{182} We develop a comprehensive multi-agent collaborative reasoning dataset using an automatic generation pipeline to improve LLM collaboration and reasoning in MAS; \ding{183} We train the M1-32B model, which exhibits strong collaborative reasoning abilities; \ding{184} We propose an adaptive scaling strategy that incorporates a CEO agent powered by M1-32B to dynamically guide multi-agent collaboration and reasoning; and \ding{185} We demonstrate through extensive experiments that our method significantly outperforms baseline models and achieves performance comparable to DeepSeek-R1 on certain tasks.
