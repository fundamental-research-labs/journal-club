# Notes

## Why It Matters
MoA is a strong example of model-level composition: instead of training a larger model, it coordinates several existing LLMs through a structured proposer/aggregator pipeline. Its main lesson for the multi-agent corpus is that agent collaboration can work when interaction is constrained to useful intermediate artifacts and synthesis prompts, rather than open-ended conversation.

## When To Cite
Cite for prompt-only LLM ensembling, multi-agent response synthesis, proposer/aggregator architectures, open-source models outperforming a closed-model baseline on AlpacaEval 2.0 in a 2024 comparison, and discussions of quality versus latency/cost in multi-LLM inference.

## Key Terms
Mixture-of-Agents (MoA); proposer; aggregator; aggregate-and-synthesize prompt; layered ensemble; model-level mixture-of-experts analogy; AlpacaEval 2.0; length-controlled win rate; MT-Bench; FLASK; MoA-Lite; time to first token (TTFT).
