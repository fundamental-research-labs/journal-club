# Notes

## Why It Matters
This paper is a canonical bridge between social-dilemma game theory and deep multi-agent reinforcement learning. Its core contribution is the SSD framing: cooperation and defection are not labels on single actions, but learned policies whose difficulty and coordination requirements shape the resulting social outcome.

## When To Cite
Cite it when discussing sequential social dilemmas, Markov-game models of cooperation and conflict, empirical game-theoretic analysis of learned policies, independent DQN agents in multi-agent environments, or why matrix-game abstractions can miss important policy-learning effects.

## Key Terms
Sequential social dilemma; matrix-game social dilemma; Markov game; empirical payoff matrix; empirical game-theoretic analysis; Gathering; Wolfpack; beam-use rate; aggressiveness; lone-wolf capture rate; deep Q-network; independent learners; cooperation policy; defection policy; resource scarcity; group capture benefit.
