# Claims

## Claim 1
**Claim:** The AI co-scientist operationalizes hypothesis generation as a scientist-in-the-loop, multi-agent generate-review-rank-evolve loop rather than a single-pass literature summarizer.

**Evidence:** Section 3 describes a Gemini 2.0 system with a natural language interface, asynchronous task framework, context memory, and specialized Generation, Reflection, Ranking, Evolution, Proximity, and Meta-review agents. Figure 2 shows the supervisor-managed task queue and feedback loop; Section 3.4 describes ways scientists can refine goals, add manual reviews, and contribute their own hypotheses.

**Caveats/Scope:** The architecture is described and evaluated as a research system, not as a fully autonomous scientific workflow. Final scientific decisions remain under expert oversight.

**Source pointers:** `paper.pdf`, Sections 3.1-3.5; Figure 2

## Claim 2
**Claim:** Scaling test-time compute appears to improve co-scientist hypothesis quality under the paper's Elo-based auto-evaluation.

**Evidence:** Section 4.2 reports upward Elo trends over ten temporal buckets across 203 research goals, and a similar trend on 15 expert-curated biomedical goals. Figure 5 compares the co-scientist with Gemini 2.0, OpenAI o1/o3-mini-high, DeepSeek R1, and expert "best guess" baselines under the same tournament metric.

**Caveats/Scope:** Elo is generated by the system's own evaluation process and is not independent ground truth. The paper explicitly warns that this metric may favor attributes that do not match expert preferences, quality, or accuracy.

**Source pointers:** `paper.pdf`, Sections 4.1-4.2; Figures 3-6

## Claim 3
**Claim:** Human experts judged co-scientist outputs as comparatively promising in a small evaluation, but the evidence is preliminary.

**Evidence:** For 11 expert-evaluated research goals, Section 4.3 reports that co-scientist outputs had the best average preference rank among the compared systems and higher novelty and impact ratings than the listed baselines. Figure 7 presents the expert preference, novelty, and impact ratings.

**Caveats/Scope:** The study is small, subjective, and domain-specific. It does not establish objective correctness or generalize across all scientific areas.

**Source pointers:** `paper.pdf`, Section 4.3; Figure 7

## Claim 4
**Claim:** The AML drug-repurposing case provides preliminary wet-lab support for some co-scientist-generated hypotheses.

**Evidence:** Section 4.5 reports expert review of co-scientist drug-repurposing proposals and in vitro AML experiments. Five expert-selected drugs with existing evidence were tested, with Binimetinib, Pacritinib, and Cerivastatin inhibiting MOLM-13 viability. For novel candidates, KIRA6 inhibited cell viability in KG-1, MOLM-13, and HL-60 cells, with reported nM-range IC50s.

**Caveats/Scope:** These are cell-line viability assays and candidate-selection studies, not in vivo efficacy, clinical validation, or regulatory evidence. Expert filtering and selection shape what was tested.

**Source pointers:** `paper.pdf`, Sections 4.5.1-4.5.2; Figures 9-11; Appendix Section A.5.4

## Claim 5
**Claim:** The liver-fibrosis case suggests the system can propose experimentally actionable epigenetic target hypotheses.

**Evidence:** Section 4.6 says experts selected three of fifteen top-ranked co-scientist hypotheses about epigenetic modifiers in liver fibrosis. Drugs targeting two of the three suggested modifiers showed significant anti-fibrotic activity in human hepatic organoids without causing cellular toxicity in the reported assay; Figure 12 summarizes the organoid results.

**Caveats/Scope:** The paper does not disclose all target/drug identities in the main text and says details will appear in an upcoming report. Organoid results are an early biological validation, not clinical evidence.

**Source pointers:** `paper.pdf`, Section 4.6; Figure 12

## Claim 6
**Claim:** The cf-PICI antimicrobial-resistance case demonstrates recapitulation of an unpublished mechanism from limited public-context inputs.

**Evidence:** Section 4.7 states that researchers gave the system a single-page background document and two relevant papers, then asked why cf-PICIs are found across diverse bacterial species. The top-ranked co-scientist hypothesis proposed that cf-PICIs interact with diverse phage tails to expand host range, matching an independent co-timed experimental study that was not public during hypothesis generation.

**Caveats/Scope:** This is a recapitulation case rather than proof of fully independent discovery from scratch. The system had curated background inputs and open literature access, and detailed validation is deferred to a companion report.

**Source pointers:** `paper.pdf`, Section 4.7; Figure 13; References 20-21
