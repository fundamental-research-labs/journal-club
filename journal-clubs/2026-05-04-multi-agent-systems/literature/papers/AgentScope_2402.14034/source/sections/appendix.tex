\section{Running Example of Standalone Conversation}
\label{app:conv}

\lstref{lst:conv_history} shows the dialog history when running the standalone conversation. 

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Dialog, float={th}, caption={A running example of standalone conversation.}, label={lst:conv_history}]
Assistant: Thank you! I'm here to help. How can I assist you today?
User: I wanna know about large language models. 
Assistant: Large language models are AI models trained on vast amounts of text data to understand and generate human-like text.
User: What's the most famous and powerful LLM in the world? 
Assistant: The most famous and powerful large language model currently is OpenAI's GPT-4.
User: What about the other LLMs?
Assistant: There are several other notable large language models including:

1. BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) by Google: This model is designed to understand the context of words in a sentence by looking at what's before and after them.

2. T5 (Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer) also by Google: This model treats every NLP problem as a text generation task.

3. RoBERTa by Facebook: This is a variant of BERT and is trained on more data and for a longer amount of time.

4. XLNet by Google Brain: This model overcomes some of the limitations of BERT by taking into account all possible word sequences in a sentence.

5. Turing-NLG by Microsoft: This is a 17-billion parameter language model by Microsoft, designed for various NLP tasks.

6. Megatron by NVIDIA: This is designed to train Transformer models with billions of parameters. 

These models are used for a variety of NLP tasks including translation, question answering, summarization, and more.
User: Okay, what should I know about LLM? 
Assistant: Large Language Models (LLMs) are powerful AI tools that can generate human-like text, but they require careful handling due to potential risks like generating harmful or biased content.
User: Okay, thanks.
Assistant: You're welcome! If you have any other questions, feel free to ask.
User: exit
\end{lstlisting}
