# Thesis sentence audit — September 18, 2026

Reviewed the complete pre-edit thesis: 46 prose paragraphs across six sections, excluding headings and the footer. Flagged 38 passages in 30 paragraphs (65%), with examples in every section. A passage can contain more than one sentence; these are editorial judgments, not a measured percentage of empty sentences. An affected paragraph can still contain substantial evidence.

The problem is widespread in transitions and conclusions, rather than an absence of factual content throughout the thesis. The result paragraphs generally supply mechanisms, comparators, numbers, and qualifications. The recurring defects are:

- **Empty signpost:** announces importance, complexity, or the next example without naming the reason or question.
- **Redundant:** repeats a nearby claim without adding evidence, an inference, a necessary connection, or a consequence.
- **Underspecified:** contains a useful idea but leaves its actor, comparison, condition, or proposed check too vague.

Broad statements are not automatically empty. For example, “A successful attempt may depend on circumstances that will never recur” identifies the generalization problem, and “Equal attempt counts do not imply equal total costs” identifies a specific comparability limit. The revision preserves those ideas. A sentence need not contain a paper name or a number to earn its place.
