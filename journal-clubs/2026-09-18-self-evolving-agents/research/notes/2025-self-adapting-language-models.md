## Question and methods

### Question and mechanism

SEAL asks whether a model can generate a *self-edit*—synthetic training content plus optimization instructions—that produces a useful parameter update. An outer ReSTEM reinforcement loop rewards the current model for self-edits according to the downstream performance of the model after an inner SFT/LoRA update. This is direct persistent weight adaptation, but the evaluator, update implementation, task/data, and outer algorithm remain fixed.

### Experimental settings

The ARC few-shot experiment uses Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct and a deliberately curated small subset: 11 ARC training tasks for outer optimization and 8 distinct evaluation tasks. During training the model samples 15 self-edits per task; at evaluation it samples five edits per held-out task. The reported success rate is the share of those edit attempts that yields the correct answer, not accuracy over the full ARC benchmark. See §4.1, Table 1, and Appendix A.

Knowledge incorporation uses Qwen2.5-7B and SQuAD v1.1. Outer training runs two ReSTEM rounds; each uses 50 training contexts, five candidate edits per context, and three random inner-update seeds, keeping the best candidate per context. The principal evaluation subset has 200 held-out passages and 974 associated questions; GPT-4.1-2025-04-14 grades answers. Single-passage adaptation uses LoRA; the 200- and 2,067-passage continued-pretraining settings use full fine-tuning. See §4.2 and Appendix B.1–B.4.

### Resource accounting

ARC ReSTEM took roughly 2–3 hours on one A100, H100, or H200; each task-time update took from about 30 seconds to several minutes (§A.4). In knowledge incorporation, one outer round entails 50 passages × five completions × three runs = 750 inner-loop fine-tune/evaluate iterations and took about six hours on 2×H100 (§B.5); two rounds imply roughly twelve hours at that stated rate, excluding other tuning and evaluation. All headline results use tuned hyperparameters, so the search cost is not fully captured by those run times.

## Results and evidence

- On the eight-task curated ARC evaluation subset, ICL scored 0%, untrained self-edits 20%, SEAL 72.5%, and an oracle human-crafted TTT configuration 100% (Table 1). Because there are five sampled edits per task, this is 40 edit attempts over eight selected tasks; it is not an eight-item task accuracy or an ARC leaderboard result. No confidence interval is reported.
- For single-passage SQuAD incorporation, no-context accuracy was 32.7% for the base model, 33.5% after training on the raw passage, 39.7% with base-model synthetic data, 46.3% with GPT-4.1 synthetic data, and 47.0% with SEAL (Table 2). The main evaluation pool is 974 questions from 200 passages. Random-seed averaging occurs while selecting edits in outer training; the paper does not report repeat-to-repeat uncertainty for these Table 2 end scores.
- Continued pretraining on 200 passages produced 58.2% for SEAL versus 59.4% for GPT-4.1 synthetic data. On all 2,067 SQuAD validation passages, SEAL scored 46.4% versus 49.2% for GPT-4.1 synthetic data (Table 2). Thus SEAL only exceeds GPT-4.1 synthetic data in the single-passage condition.
- Sequential edits degrade earlier-task performance, establishing that the current method still forgets (Figure 6 and §5). Appendix Table 5 reports entrywise SEMs for this experiment, mostly about 0.02–0.04, but the underlying repeat denominator is not clearly stated adjacent to the table.

## Appraisal and limitations

### Authors' claim

Learning a policy for writing self-edits improves task-local adaptation and can outperform synthetic data generated by a stronger model in one setting.

### Evidence

The paper has explicit update mechanics, held-out passages/tasks, relevant baselines, some seed handling, compute detail, and a direct forgetting probe. Its strongest clean comparison is the 47.0% versus 39.7% single-passage result for learned versus untrained Qwen self-edits.

### Interpretation

This is strong teaching evidence that model-generated data can be optimized for downstream learnability. It is not evidence of open-ended recursive improvement: only two narrow domains are tested, the ARC subset is selected for solvability, the evaluation/reward apparatus is external, repeated-update retention declines, and final-score uncertainty is mostly absent. It belongs in the same broad weight-update family as AZR/R-Zero/TTRL but supplies a distinct mechanism: generating explicit update programs from external context rather than generating a curriculum or pseudo-labeling the test set.
