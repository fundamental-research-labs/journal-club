## Question and methods

SIA asks whether an automated improvement loop can obtain additional task performance by selecting between changes to an agent scaffold and changes to the task model's weights. A Claude Sonnet 4.6 Meta-Agent creates the initial task scaffold. A frozen Claude Sonnet 4.6 Feedback-Agent observes the scaffold, execution trajectory, metrics, and task specification, then requests either a scaffold rewrite or an RL update. The Task-Specific Agent is gpt-oss-120b, with rank-32 LoRA adapters for weight updates and learning rate 4×10^-5 (§§5.1–5.3; Table 2).

The claimed update selector is not learned. It uses the frozen Feedback-Agent's prior and, in all three reported tasks, begins with scaffold iteration and switches to weights when progress stalls (§6.2). The paper proposes learning the selector through outer-loop meta-RL only as future work (§9). This is therefore joint harness/weight adaptation, not demonstrated recursive improvement of the improvement policy.

## Results and evidence

### Evaluation and findings

| Task | Data/evaluator | Initial | Harness-only best | Harness + weights | Previous SOTA | Locator |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| LawBench, 191 charge classes | 5,332 train / 913 test; top-1 accuracy | 13.5% | 50.0% | 70.1% | 45.0% | Table 2; §6.3.1; Fig. 4; Table 3 |
| AlphaEvolve TriMul | One fixed input shape; H100 timing; reward 1500/runtime | 0.105 | 0.120 (12,483 µs) | 1.475 (1,017 µs) | 1.292 (1,161 µs) | Table 2; §6.3.2; Fig. 5; Table 3 |
| MAGIC scRNA denoising | Pancreas data; normalized reconstruction score against ground truth | 0.048 | 0.241 | 0.289 | 0.240 | Table 2; §6.3.3; Fig. 6; Table 3 |

The reported incremental gains beyond the harness-only operating point are +20.1 percentage points on LawBench, runtime reduction from 12,483 to 1,017 µs on TriMul, and a 0.048 absolute / 20% relative rise on denoising (§7.1). Weight-update algorithms differ by task: PPO with GAE for LawBench, entropic advantage weighting for TriMul, and GRPO for denoising (§§6.3, 7.3). The denoising improvement is attributed to generated code adding `np.clip` and `np.rint`, a post-processing heuristic the scaffold search had not proposed (§6.3.3).

## Appraisal and limitations

### Split, compute, and uncertainty audit

- **Selection/evaluation leakage:** LawBench's 913 examples are called a held-out test split, but §6.3.1 says all evaluations are on that split and that generated solution scripts are executed against it to produce rewards. The split is held out from the 5,332 training examples, but it is not held out from SIA's iterative model/scaffold selection. No untouched final split is reported.
- **Other evaluator reuse:** TriMul repeatedly optimizes one fixed shape against H100 timing. Denoising repeatedly optimizes against a score computed from the target pancreas dataset and its ground truth. No fresh shapes, datasets, or cross-domain tasks are used for post-selection validation.
- **Comparators:** the principal ablation is sequential: first-generation scaffold, best harness-only point, then weights added after harness plateau (§6.2). There is no weight-only arm, fixed human-authored training recipe, random action selector, matched search method, or comparison of equal total resources across operators.
- **Uncertainty:** no number of independent runs, seeds, standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests is reported for Table 3 or Figures 4–6.
- **Compute/cost:** H100s via Modal are stated (§4.3), as are LoRA rank and learning rate. Generation counts, rollout group sizes, optimizer steps, H100-hours, token counts, wall time, and dollar cost are not reported. The paper describes additional RL algorithms observed in unreported tasks (§7.3), so those descriptions are not evidence from the three displayed experiments.
- **Persistence/regression:** no evaluation tests retention after further updates, transfer to new tasks, broad capability regressions, catastrophic forgetting, or robustness to changed scaffolds/verifiers.

### Authors' claim

Harness edits shape search and execution, while weight updates internalize domain knowledge unavailable to scaffold edits; together they outperform harness-only optimization across three domains.

### Evidence-supported narrow claim

In these three evaluator-driven optimization runs, the selected post-weight-update endpoint scores higher than the best reported scaffold-only endpoint under the same task evaluator.

### Interpretation

This is the clearest current demonstration of one orchestrator invoking both scaffold and LoRA update machinery. It is not clean evidence that the weight changes generalize: selection and reporting share the same evaluator, most sharply through repeated use of LawBench's nominal test split. The denoising “internalized biological constraint” interpretation is also stronger than the evidence; the visible outcome is that a fine-tuned code generator emitted a two-line rounding/clipping heuristic on the optimized dataset.

The authors themselves identify coupled Goodhart risk: both update levers optimize the same fixed verifier, and the joint equilibrium may be fragile to new scaffolds or policies (§8). That limitation should lead any presentation of the headline gains.
