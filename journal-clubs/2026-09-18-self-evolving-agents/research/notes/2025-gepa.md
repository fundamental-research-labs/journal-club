## Question and methods

### Question and method

Can natural-language reflection evolve prompts for compound AI systems more sample-efficiently than weight-space RL or other prompt optimizers? GEPA samples traces, diagnoses failures, proposes prompt mutations, retains candidates on an instance-wise Pareto frontier, and sometimes merges complementary descendants. The optimizer sees labeled training instances and monitors a content-hidden validation set; final evaluation is held out (§§3–4, Algorithm 1).

### Tasks, splits, and resources

Six tasks use Qwen3-8B and GPT-4.1-mini: HotpotQA, IFBench, HoVer, PUPA, AIME-2025, and LiveBench-Math (§4; Appendix E.1). HotpotQA uses 150/300/300 train/validation/test; IFBench uses 150/300/294, with unseen constraint types in test; AIME uses 45/45 questions from 2022–2024 for train/validation and the 30 AIME-2025 problems repeated five times for test; LiveBench-Math uses 368 questions shuffled with seed 0 and split approximately equally (Appendix E.1). Table 1 reports task-dependent GEPA budgets of 1,839–7,051 rollouts versus 24,000 for GRPO. These are rollout counts, not matched wall-clock, token, GPU, or dollar budgets; most GEPA rollouts are validation evaluations (§4, Observation 1).

## Results and evidence

### Reported evidence

With Qwen3-8B, Table 1 reports aggregate 45.23 baseline, 48.91 GRPO, 47.84 MIPROv2, and 54.85 GEPA. GEPA exceeds GRPO on five of six tasks but loses on AIME-2025 (32.0 versus 38.0). It reaches 62.33 on HotpotQA versus 43.33 GRPO and 38.61 on IFBench versus 35.88 GRPO, using 6,871 and 3,593 rollouts respectively. With GPT-4.1-mini, Table 2 reports aggregate 65.22 GEPA versus 58.67 MIPROv2 and 53.03 baseline; prompts optimized on Qwen transfer to 62.03. Table 3's fixed-harness selection ablation reports aggregate gain +12.44 for Pareto selection versus +6.05 greedy and +5.11 beam search on four tasks. Main tables give point estimates without repeated-run uncertainty or confidence intervals.

### Coordinator arithmetic audit (September 16)

Table 1’s six displayed GRPO task scores (43.33, 35.88, 38.67, 86.66, 38.00, 51.26) average **48.9667**, whereas the reported aggregate is **48.91**. Rounding each input to two decimals cannot explain the approximately 0.057-point discrepancy. ## Appraisal and limitations

### Authors' claim

Rich textual feedback lets reflective prompt evolution outperform GRPO and prior prompt optimizers with fewer rollouts.

### Interpretation and limitations

This is strong evidence that context can be a high-bandwidth adaptation surface under structured, labeled tasks. It does not establish cheaper adaptation in total compute: GEPA invokes reflection models and repeatedly scores validation candidates, while GRPO changes weights. The six tasks are heterogeneous and the aggregate is an unweighted mean of task scores. AIME's repeated test questions do not create 150 independent items. Optimizer stochasticity is not summarized across independent runs. The later VISTA paper is a direct challenge: reflective evolution can degrade from defective seeds, so GEPA should teach search mechanics and failure sensitivity rather than monotonic self-improvement.
