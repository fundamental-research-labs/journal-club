# Gödel Machines

## Source and access

Key: `2003-goedel-machines`. Jürgen Schmidhuber. *Gödel Machines: Self-Referential Universal Problem Solvers Making Provably Optimal Self-Improvements*. [Primary v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0309048v5), December 17, 2006; first September 25, 2003, with published chapter variants listed on the canonical record. Read September 16, 2026: §§2.2/3.2/4.1–4.4 and selected citation contexts. Temporary PDF inspected; not retained under arXiv's assumed non-exclusive distribution license. This is theoretical background, not an empirical agent experiment.

## Question and methods

The system includes a proof searcher and a candidate self-rewrite. The rewrite executes after a proof establishes greater expected utility from switching than from continuing the proof search, relative to encoded axioms, utility and hardware/resource assumptions (§2.2 and §3.2). The proof searcher is itself potentially editable.

## Results and evidence

Theorem 4.1 concerns the advantage of switching over waiting for the same proof process to discover alternatives. It is not an unconditional guarantee that a practical agent will find the best program; finding useful proofs can be difficult (§4.4). No numerical result is imported into the thesis.

## Appraisal and limitations

DGM substitutes empirical benchmark evaluation and archive exploration for this proof-gated criterion (DGM §3). The name therefore establishes an intellectual relationship, not transfer of the original optimality guarantee. The essay uses this source only to clarify that distinction.
