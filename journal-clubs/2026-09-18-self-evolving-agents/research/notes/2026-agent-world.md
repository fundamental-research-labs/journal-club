## Question and methods

Agent-World asks whether an automated pipeline can build a diverse executable environment curriculum and then repeatedly target an agent's observed weaknesses. It collects about 3,500 seed themes from MCP server specifications, tool documentation, and industrial product-requirement documents. GPT-OSS-120B agents mine web-grounded databases, generate and test tools, synthesize tasks, and diagnose policy failures (§3). After filtering, the paper reports 1,978 environments and 19,822 distinct tools, averaging more than ten tools per environment (§3.1.1; Fig. 4).

Task synthesis has two branches. Graph-based synthesis builds dependency graphs over tools, samples executable paths, derives answers by sandbox execution, and asks an LLM to construct a rubric. Programmatic synthesis generates and debugs solution code and executable validators. In both cases, a ReAct agent attempts each generated task five times; a task is retained when at least two runs succeed (§3.1.1). This filter establishes solvability under one agent configuration, but it also selects tasks by that agent's behavior and does not validate rubric correctness through human review.

Training begins with 40,000 cold-start SFT trajectories generated by an in-house Doubao-Seed-1.8 policy, followed by 5,000 synthesized RL samples for Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B. GRPO uses batches of 32 tasks and eight rollouts per task, with trajectories up to 80K tokens and individual generations capped at 32K (§4.1). Graph tasks use an LLM judge over rubric criteria; programmatic tasks use sandboxed validators (§3.2.1).

The “self-evolving arena” samples five environments from each of 20 top-level categories. In each round it creates fresh evaluation tasks, diagnoses failure traces with GPT-OSS-120B, complexifies weak environments, generates targeted training tasks, and continues RL (§3.2.2; Algorithm 1). The editable persistent object is the Qwen policy weights; the external curriculum and databases also change. The improvement recipe and diagnosis model remain fixed.

## Results and evidence

### Evaluation and reported results

The authors evaluate on 23 benchmarks using an in-house harness. They state that some benchmarks, including GAIA and HLE, use sampled subsets “to accelerate evaluation,” but do not report subset sizes, item identifiers, or sampling seeds (§4.1). All experiments are said to be repeated eight times and Table 1 reports average accuracy, although no standard deviations or intervals are shown.

Main tool-use results (Table 1):

| Model | MCP-Mark | BFCL V4 | τ²-Bench |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Qwen3-8B backbone | 2.4 | 40.4 | 26.2 |
| EnvScaler-8B | 5.6 | 47.6 | 37.9 |
| Agent-World-8B | 8.9 | 51.4 | 61.8 |
| Qwen3-14B backbone | 3.4 | 41.0 | 32.4 |
| AWM-14B | 5.1 | 42.4 | 39.0 |
| Agent-World-14B | 13.3 | 55.8 | 65.4 |

The environment-count sweep increases training environments from 0 to 10, 100, 500, 1,000, and 1,978. Across four selected domains, the plotted average rises from 18.4% to 38.5%, with diminishing marginal gains after 500 (§4.3.3; Fig. 8). This is an environment-scale association under the paper's training pipeline; compute and number of examples also plausibly grow with environment count, so it does not isolate diversity from training resources.

Table 2 reports two targeted evolution rounds:

| Starting model | Base τ² / BFCL / MCP-Postgres | Round 1 | Round 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agent-World-14B | 60.2 / 52.4 / 29.5 | 63.5 / 54.9 / 36.3 | 65.4 / 55.8 / 38.1 |
| EnvScaler-8B | 37.9 / 47.6 / 9.5 | 40.2 / 49.1 / 13.9 | 41.6 / 50.0 / 15.1 |

The surrounding prose instead says Agent-World-14B moves from **45.3/52.4/29.5 to 50.5/55.8/38.1**, conflicting with the table's τ² values while agreeing on BFCL and MCP-Mark (§4.3.4). The table should be cited with this internal inconsistency visible, not silently reconciled.

Figures 6–7 claim improvements across reasoning, search/coding, MCP, SkillsBench, ARC-AGI-2, and Claw-Eval. The figures provide broader transfer evidence, but many values are graphical only, and no statistical uncertainty is provided. “Without benchmark-specific training” does not establish absence of related web/database content in the automatically mined training ecosystem.

## Appraisal and limitations

### Split, cost, and evaluator audit

- **Training versus external evaluation:** the named 23 benchmarks are separate from the synthesized RL tasks, which is better than optimizing the benchmark directly. However, the system mines web content and themes at scale, and the paper does not report task-level overlap, environment-name filtering, contamination checks, or benchmark-date controls.
- **Internal arena:** arena environments are called held out from initial training, but they are used to diagnose weaknesses and generate targeted training in later rounds. They are therefore development environments within the loop, not untouched final tests. Fresh tasks reduce literal item reuse but share generators, tools, databases, and validators.
- **Evaluator independence:** generated tools and their tests come from the same coding agent; generated tasks, solutions, rubrics, and validators share LLM generation pipelines. Graph-task RL additionally relies on an LLM judge. The five-attempt task filter checks agent solvability, not validator validity or absence of exploitable artifacts.
- **Comparator fairness:** “same training setting” is asserted for environment-scaling methods, but Agent-World includes private 40K Doubao-generated SFT trajectories plus 5K RL samples. The paper gives no matched token, trajectory, GPU-hour, wall-time, or dollar budget across methods. Environment scaling sweeps likewise do not hold data or compute constant.
- **Resources:** rollout batch and maximum lengths are given, but optimizer steps, per-round sample counts, training hardware, GPU-hours, inference tokens, environment-generation cost, and total cost are not reported.
- **Uncertainty:** eight repetitions are stated, but no per-run data, standard deviations, intervals, or tests appear. Table 2 does not state whether each round result is an eight-run mean.
- **Reproducibility:** private SFT generation, an in-house evaluation harness, unspecified benchmark subsets, and no identified release prevent a close reproduction from the paper alone.

### Authors' claim

Automatically discovered executable environments and targeted repeated RL yield broad, continuous agent improvement and policy-environment co-evolution.

### Evidence-supported narrow claim

Under the authors' private data and in-house evaluation pipeline, the Agent-World checkpoints score above the listed same-scale environment-training baselines on the three main tool-use suites, and reported scores increase over two targeted RL rounds. The results support environment/curriculum generation as a promising training mechanism.

### Interpretation

This is a substantial and unusually broad environment-generation system. It does not establish autonomous open-ended evolution: GPT-OSS-120B runs a fixed, human-designed mining/diagnosis recipe, each round receives new generated data and RL compute, and the improvement rate diminishes after two rounds. The external benchmark breadth is valuable, but opaque private inputs, unmatched resources, absent uncertainty, and the Table 2 inconsistency weaken causal attribution.

## Discussion and follow-up

### Shortlist decision

Promote from unreviewed lead to **reviewed reserve**. It fills autonomous environment/curriculum generation better than the current corpus, but does **not** merit displacing a stronger top-ten evidence family on present evidence. If mechanism coverage requires one curriculum example, use Agent-World as the qualified representative and replace only the lowest-evidence redundant framing source, never a controlled evaluation or longitudinal/generalization source.
