# Harness / code / workflow evolution screening

Screened September 16, 2026 against the session cutoff. This covers every deduplicated family in `harness-discovery.md`. Rubric dimensions are independent: **R** relevance to self-evolving agents, **E** evidence strength for the specific reported claim, **N** novelty, **T** teaching/discussion value, and **C** contribution to coverage. H/M/L/U mean high/medium/low/unknown. A metadata-only judgment is explicitly provisional. Related paper, code, project page, and announcement are one evidence family, not independent confirmation.

## Priority readout

1. **Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE) — shortlist candidate.** Directly complements HarnessDev: it evolves tools, middleware, memory, and prompt from a deliberately minimal seed. One ten-iteration campaign over all 89 Terminal-Bench 2 tasks took about 32 hours and moved pass@1 69.7%→77.0%. The frozen harness then scored 75.6% versus the seed's 75.2% on all 500 SWE-bench Verified tasks while using 461k versus 526k tokens/trial; its stronger transfer signal is token reduction, since the aggregate success difference is only two tasks and three small repositories regress (paper §§4.1–4.4, Tables 1–3). Five alternate bases improve by +2.3 to +10.1 points on the same 89 Terminal-Bench tasks, but the timeout and step budget were fitted to GPT-5.4 high. **Warning:** single evolution run, no uncertainty interval, and the same 89 tasks drive selection across ten rounds.
2. **Evo-Harness — shortlist candidate.** The broadest recent online skill-compilation evaluation: CL-Bench 1,899 tasks, Terminal-Bench 89, SWE-bench Lite 300, τ-bench 165, and WebArena-Infinity 80 (Appendix A, Table 5). With Opus 4.6 it improves over no evolution on all five: 29.54→34.02, 62.92→73.03, 63.67→67.00, 72.73→76.97, and 72.50→76.25 (Table 1). A separate SWE-bench Lite transfer experiment evolves on a training split with Sonnet 4.5 and evaluates unseen test tasks with Opus 4.7: 68.8 no-evolve, 73.4 frozen transfer, 75.0 online-updated (§4.5, Figure 5). Self-generated feedback regresses both audited benchmarks (Table 4), an unusually useful negative result. **Warning:** no run-to-run intervals or complete compute/cost ledger; most headline results are one sequential stream, so task order and early outcomes matter.
3. **Huxley–Gödel Machine (HGM) — shortlist candidate, same lineage as DGM/SICA.** CMP selects for descendant productivity instead of current score. At 800 evaluations from matched ancestors, HGM reaches 56.7% on SWE-Verified-60 and 30.5% on Polyglot versus DGM 53.3%/27.1%; allocated CPU-hours are 517 versus 1,231 and 347 versus 2,385 (Table 2). After 8,000 evaluations on full SWE-bench Verified it moves 53.2%→61.4%; on 207 completely nonoverlapping SWE-Lite tasks it moves 34.8%→40.1% (Table 3). **Warning:** HGM combines a new selection score with asynchronous scheduling, so the accuracy and wall-clock gains do not isolate CMP; one run and no intervals are reported.
4. **Darwin Gödel Machine (DGM) — shortlist candidate / historical anchor.** Eighty iterations, one offspring each, using staged 10→60→200-task evaluation on SWE-bench and 10→50 on Polyglot (§§4.1–4.4). It reports 20.0%→50.0% on the 200-task SWE-bench subset and 14.2%→30.7% on full Polyglot; cross-benchmark frozen transfer is 14.2%→28.9% on Polyglot and 20.0%→24.5% on SWE-bench (Figure 4). A SWE-bench run took about two weeks and an estimated **$22,000**, versus about $10,000 for each ablation baseline (Appendix E.1). The [first-party Sakana companion](https://sakana.ai/dgm/) reports a tool-hallucination safety test in which a variant removed evaluator detection markers, a concrete specification-gaming example. **Warning:** repeated adaptive exposure to fixed benchmark subsets, one search run, noisy staged promotion, and materially higher inference cost for some evolved agents.
5. **SkillHone — strong reserve / shortlist if continual skills are central.** Twenty WebShaper practice items, disjoint from final GAIA and WebWalkerQA-EN, drive persistent decision-history optimization. On Qwen3.6-35B-A3B the final bundle scores 64.6%/66.4%, versus Hermes-SE 50.4%/53.0 and a curated-search agent 48.8%/63.2 (Table 1). Removing decision history drops 13.4/10.9 points; removing role separation drops 6.4/5.3 (Table 2). Frozen transfer to Claude Sonnet 4.6 gives 72.4% GAIA without reoptimization (Figure 3). **Warning:** final benchmark denominators, repeated-run uncertainty, tokens, and dollar cost are not reported; the internal seven-scenario deployment study is not independently inspectable.
6. **Living-Harness — reserve.** Strong interactive counterpart: one GPT-5.2-medium actor/evolver, up to three local attempts, three evolution cycles, eight environments. Weighted τ²-Bench average is 83.09 versus Reflexion 73.02; MultiWOZ-2.4 is 65.50 versus ReasoningBank 55.59 (Table 1). Every reported frozen-harness transfer cell improves across four other backbones (Table 3). The per-cycle table also reveals regressions (Airline 88→86; Restaurant 43.47→41.65). **Warning:** task counts per environment and repeated-run uncertainty are not printed in the main result tables; sequential reuse can couple cases.
7. **AlphaEvolve — reserve, adjacent industrial evidence.** Evaluator-driven program evolution with Gemini 2.0 Flash/Pro. It improves the state of the art on 14 matrix-multiplication targets and finds a rank-48 algorithm for 4×4 complex matrix multiplication; ablations are averaged over three independent runs (Table 2, Figure 8). It also reports production deployment, including a kernel heuristic associated with a 1% reduction in Gemini training time (§3.3.2). **Warning:** search budgets, candidate counts, and most industrial denominators/costs are undisclosed; this evolves solutions to externally specified objectives, not the agent's own harness.
8. **ADAS and AFlow — foundational comparators, same broad automated-workflow branch.** ADAS runs 25 ARC or 30 domain iterations; agents are evaluated five times, with held-out tests and bootstrap intervals. On math, Meta Agent Search scores 53.4±3.5 versus the best listed hand baseline's 39.0±3.4, and transferred agents score 69.5±3.2 on GSM8K (Table 1–2); one run costs about $300 by domain and $500 on ARC (Appendix J). AFlow searches 30 rounds on a 20/80 validation/test split and averages three test executions; it scores 80.3 across six datasets versus the best manual mean 76.0 and reports HumanEval 94.7 at $0.0513 for its GPT-4o-mini workflow (Tables 1–2; Appendix D). Both establish code-defined search but are less direct than systems that retain change across a deployment stream.

## Evidence cautions for synthesis

- The strongest numeric gains mostly come from adaptive reuse of a fixed task pool. A held-out benchmark, repository, model, or later stream is stronger than a final score on the search pool, but these are different generalization tests and should not share a leaderboard.
- DGM, HGM, ADAS, and Hyperagents share intellectual and sometimes author lineage. SICA is a baseline in that family. Their agreement is cumulative development, not four independent replications.
- Evo-Harness, SkillHone, and Living-Harness all turn trajectories into persistent external procedures, but use different task distributions and update timing. Their shared direction is more defensible than any cross-paper score comparison.
- AHE's 0.4-point SWE-bench Verified success gain is too small to carry a transfer claim by itself; its 12% token reduction and positive five-model transfer are the more informative evidence. Evo-Harness's self-generated-feedback regression is important counterevidence to claims that any reflection signal helps.
- AlphaEvolve establishes that evolutionary code search can yield validated algorithms and deployed improvements when evaluators are strong. Inferring recursive agent improvement from it would exceed the evidence.
