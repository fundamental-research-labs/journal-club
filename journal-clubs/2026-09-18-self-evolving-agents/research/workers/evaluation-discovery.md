## Replication and evaluation takeaways

1. **The key control is paired persistence:** same ordered tasks, same base model, persistent state versus wiped state, with held-out tasks/domains and total proposal/validation/inference budgets equalized. SEA-Eval and FinEvo-Bench provide longitudinal framing; CL-Bench and Continual Learning Bench offer stateful operationalizations.
2. **Final success rate is insufficient.** Track cost/token trajectories, regressions on old tasks, adaptation speed, retention, cross-stream transfer, uncertainty/abstention, and maintenance cost. SEA-Eval’s reported 31.2× token spread is a strong motivation, not yet a verified result in this lane.
3. **Evaluator integrity is part of the intervention surface.** RewardHackingAgents makes evaluator tampering and leakage explicit; RHB and gridworlds test reward/proxy exploitation. Any self-evolution system that can modify harness, tests, logs, or scoring must isolate and audit those channels.
4. **Judge-based longitudinal scores need independent checks.** FinEvo-Bench’s Claude-based scoring and many agent studies risk correlated judge/model preferences. Use blinded randomized output order, multiple judges, human calibration, objective checks where possible, and adversarial judge tests (Evaluating the Evaluator, CALM, JudgeBench).
5. **Negative results are often transfer failures rather than no learning.** Evo-Bench’s abstract-level pattern—Search/General gains but Office weakness and early saturation—pairs naturally with the existing harness-budget critique. Claims should name task family, model, search budget, and held-out split.
6. **Domain expansion changes what “improvement” means.** Finance requires compliance and procedure adherence; embodied tasks require action-interface transfer and safety; CRM requires state mutation and entity consistency; code requires maintainability and future-change cost. One aggregate score cannot stand in for all four.

## Counterevidence and cautions

- Positive benchmark results can be real while still being narrow: FinEvo-Bench’s repeated within-scene gains support retained experience under its procedures, but do not establish open-world professional competence.
- A lower score after benchmark evolution can mean harder or distribution-shifted tasks, not model regression; dynamic benchmark generators need verified difficulty matching and item-quality audits.
- Reward-hacking rates depend strongly on tool affordances, sandbox permissions, training, and chain length. They should not be generalized from RHB or gridworlds to all deployed agents.
- LLM-judge bias is not proof that every judge is unusable. The appropriate claim is that judge reliability must be measured for the task, output format, and optimization loop; independent checks can make it useful.
- Repeated trials over one task stream do not create independent evidence. Report seeds, stream permutations, model/API versions, stopping rules, and failed evolution proposals.
- Search snippets, Reddit, TechRadar, Hugging Face summaries, and survey pages were discovery aids only. They are not primary support for quantitative claims above where the canonical paper was not read.

## Top five complementary additions to the session

1. **SEA-Eval** — makes the central distinction between episodic competence and long-term evolutionary trajectory visible through success/cost curves.
2. **RewardHackingAgents + RHB** — a paired evaluator-integrity and tool-use exploit counterexample to naive “score improved ⇒ agent improved.”
3. **FinEvo-Bench** — brings longitudinal evaluation into a high-stakes professional workflow with compliance outcomes and paired controls.
4. **SHAPER** — tests whether skill+harness evolution transfers into embodied settings while weights and action interfaces are constrained.
5. **Evo-Bench plus the existing harness-evaluation critique** — complementary evidence on cross-domain transfer, overfitting, budget controls, saturation, and the possibility that “evolution” is merely more search.
