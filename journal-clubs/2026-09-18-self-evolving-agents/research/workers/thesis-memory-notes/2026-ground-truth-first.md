# Ground Truth First: longitudinal memory evaluation

**Provisional worker record, superseded for thesis use by the shared notes/register and [Tier 2 corrections](../thesis-additions-screening.md).**


Source: Quentin Spencer, arXiv:2607.21962 (first 2026-07-24), https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962. Read 2026-09-16 via full arXiv HTML, abstract/introduction, related work, §§5.4–5.8, and appendices A–B. No local copy retained; license/redistribution basis not verified.

The instrument seeds facts with validity intervals, volatility, channel/provenance before rendering conversations, then compares five memory architectures plus no-memory under fixed answerer, versioned judge, three replicates, and short/tenure horizons (§1, lines 74–85; contributions lines 100–122). Curated-map recall on early content drops 96.3% to 72.2% by week 9; graph rises to 90.4% and layered hybrid to 93.2% (§1, lines 87–92; Table 5 lines 456–465). The hybrid reaches 96.8% short-horizon, while full rendered history reaches 97.9% and is statistically indistinguishable under the primary judge; at week 9 there is no judge-independent accuracy winner, although memory reads fewer tokens (§5.8, lines 315–323; Appendix B lines 475–486).

The paper also reports weak-written facts failing 24% versus 2% for clean writes, but explicitly treats this as association because writer procedure/compute and evaluator model are confounded (§5.5, lines 278–282). Injection tests are planted and non-adaptive (§5.8, lines 358–358), and the synthetic corpus plus development-tuned hybrid limit deployment extrapolation.

Interpretation: this is a consequential challenge to short-horizon memory claims and a template for tenure, full-history, recency, provenance, and cost controls. It does not show agents learning skills or accelerating; it shows why lasting value must be assessed over changing validity, old-content retention, and input-token cost.
