# Weights and learning-process Tier 2 screening

Screened September 16, 2026 against the repository cutoff. Scope is every explicit candidate and prose-only evidence lead in `weights-discovery.md`. Codes are **H/M/L/U** for high/medium/low/unknown. Ratings are separate dimensions: relevance (R), evidence strength (E), novelty (N), teaching/discussion value (T), and contribution to topic coverage (C). Evidence is **U** when primary material was not inspected beyond metadata/abstract. “Reserve” means useful evidence but not a first-choice family for an approximately ten-family topic-wide shortlist.

## Priorities and strongest judgments

1. **SEAL — shortlist candidate (H/H/H/H/H).** The cleanest direct example of a model learning to write parameter-update data/instructions. Full v2 paper inspected. Its evidence is bounded: selected 11/8-task ARC setup, 200-passage/974-question SQuAD evaluation, two outer rounds, tuned hyperparameters, and observed forgetting. Use Table 2 and §5 rather than secondary ARC claims.
2. **Absolute Zero — shortlist candidate (H/M/H/H/H).** Broad and technically concrete self-play demonstration, but “zero data” excludes curated reasoning data only; pretrained knowledge, generated seed buffers, hand-designed code environments, rewards, and verifiers remain. No repeat uncertainty and incomplete GPU-hour accounting. Pair with the self-play dynamics paper rather than present alone.
3. **R-Zero — shortlist candidate (H/M/H/H/H).** Best within-family source for Challenger/Solver separation, pseudo-label decay, and eventual performance collapse. Internal aggregate inconsistencies (Table 1 versus prose/abstract), no repeat uncertainty, and missing compute lower confidence.
4. **TTRL — reserve/conditional shortlist (H/M/H/H/H).** Important test-time/weight-update bridge, but it adapts and evaluates on the same test inputs. The 12.9→40.2 AIME result is +27.3 points / +211.6% relative on 30 questions with a 16-response pass@1 estimator, not unseen-post-adaptation generalization.
5. **Model-collapse replacement versus accumulation — shortlist as one counterevidence family.** Use Shumailov for the tail-loss failure mode (H/H/H/H/H) and Gerstgrasser et al. for the decisive boundary condition that accumulating real plus synthetic data can avoid collapse in tested settings (H/H/H/H/H). Neither establishes what happens for frontier agentic RL; together they prevent the false claim that synthetic data either inevitably collapses or is generically safe.
6. **Towards Understanding Self-play — shortlist companion (H/M/H/H/H).** Full workshop paper inspected. It finds small-k sharpening without statistically significant large-k expansion, entropy collapse, intermediate update sparsity, and a 2-point loss from altered proposer reward. It studies only AZR and two sizes, so it is analysis/counterevidence rather than an independent replication.

## Verified quantitative cautions

- **SEAL:** ARC success is 29/40 successful sampled edit attempts if 72.5% is applied to 8 tasks × 5 edits, not performance over ARC as a whole. The paper does not state that integer explicitly. SQuAD’s primary 200-passage pool contains 974 questions. One outer round is 750 inner updates and about six hours on 2×H100; two rounds roughly double that stated training time.
- **Absolute Zero:** Table 1 uses unweighted benchmark scores, then averages code and math group averages equally. The rendered table shows Qwen2.5-Coder-7B MATH-500 54.0→72.6 but labels it `+22.6`; arithmetic gives +18.6, confirming an internal source error. Greedy evaluation avoids sampling variance but does not provide training-run variance. Hardware is “clusters of A800 GPUs” for 3–5 days; accelerator count is missing. Yang Yue appears twice in the primary author metadata/list.
- **R-Zero:** visual inspection of rendered v4 Tables 1–2 confirms irreconcilable source aggregates rather than extraction errors: Table 1 Qwen3-8B is 48.64→53.72 while prose says 49.18→54.69; Table 2 is 31.98→34.50 while prose says +5.13. Prefer exact table cells and visibly flag the inconsistency. Three pseudo-label sets have n=200 each; Appendix E uses n=500 late-stage questions.
- **TTRL:** main pass@1 averages 16 stochastic outputs per question. AIME’s +211.6% relative gain is 12.9→40.2, a +27.3-point absolute change on 30 prompts. Adaptation and scoring use the same prompt set. No repeated-run uncertainty is reported.
- **Self-play dynamics:** Figure 1’s large-k degradation is stated to be statistically nonsignificant, but the main text does not give the interval/test. Figure 5 is a checkpoint parameter-difference measure, not evidence about useful retained knowledge. The altered reward lowers final validation accuracy by 2 points (Figure 6).
- **Collapse evidence:** Shumailov establishes failure under recursive replacement and finite-sample/model approximation errors. Gerstgrasser et al. establish stability under accumulation across their tested small-model/data regimes; accumulation also increases dataset size and gradient steps, though matched-size synthetic-replacement ablations still degrade. Neither result alone decides verified/filtering-heavy self-play.

## Coverage judgment

The lane now covers direct learned self-edits, autonomous task generation, co-evolving proposer/solver roles, test-set RL, learned curricula, continual-learning alternatives, frozen-weight alternatives, and both collapse and anti-collapse results. The principal remaining evidence gap is independent replication under matched compute and a genuinely future held-out stream. The strongest unresolved candidates are the v6 reinforcement-forgetting paper and the ICLR 2026 verification paper; both remain watchlisted because full primary results were not audited in this bounded pass.
