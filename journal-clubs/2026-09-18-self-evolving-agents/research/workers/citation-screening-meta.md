# Meta-search citation screening: EvoX and MLEvolve

**Screening date/cutoff:** 2026-09-16 (America/Los_Angeles). Both sources received primary full-text review (arXiv experimental HTML and PDF), including methods and main results. No paper was downloaded into the repository; temporary PDFs were used outside the workspace.

## EvoX: Meta-Evolution for Automated Discovery

**Metadata and access.** Shu Liu, Shubham Agarwal, Monishwaran Maheswaran, Mert Cemri, Zhifei Li, Qiuyang Mang, Ashwin Naren, Ethan Boneh, Audrey Cheng, Melissa Z. Pan, Alexander Du, Kurt Keutzer, Alvin Cheung, Alexandros G. Dimakis, Koushik Sen, Matei Zaharia, and Ion Stoica (UC Berkeley; Boneh Stanford; Dimakis Bespoke Labs). [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23413), [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23413v2), [v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23413v2). First submitted 2026-02-26 (v1); latest version read v2, revised 2026-03-16. ArXiv marks the paper CC BY 4.0 ([license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)); code is linked at [SkyDiscover](https://github.com/skydiscover-ai/skydiscover). Download URL for coordinator: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23413v2`.

**Question and method (~200 words).** EvoX asks whether the search strategy governing LLM evolutionary optimization can adapt during a run. Its two coupled loops retain a candidate database while an LLM generator proposes solutions. Every window of `W` evaluations is scored by best-score improvement; if improvement falls below stagnation threshold `tau`, a strategy generator mutates a high-performing prior strategy conditioned on strategy history and a population descriptor. Strategies specify parent selection, inspiration candidates, and variation operator (local refinement, structural variation, or free-form variation). Candidate strategies are syntax/validity checked and replace the active strategy without resetting the solution population (Algorithm 1; §§3–4.3, pp. 5–8). The evaluator remains task-specific and externally fixed; GPT-5 generates strategy updates, while GPT-5 or Gemini-3.0-Pro is the backbone in reported comparisons. The paper evaluates 196 problems: 8 mathematical, 6 systems, 10 ALE-Bench-Lite, and 172 Frontier-CS (Appendix A, p. 23). Open frameworks use 100 iterations per task; Tables 2–3 report means and best values over three independent runs for the math/system comparisons. Frontier-CS reports one aggregate mean/median in Figure 3; no run dispersion is supplied there. The principal claim is search-strategy adaptation, not weight or evaluator self-improvement.

**Key evidence and locators.** On eight math tasks, EvoX is best/tied-best on 7/8 with GPT-5 and all 8 with Gemini by the authors’ count; mean wins are 6/8 and 7/8 respectively (Table 2, §6.1, pp. 11–12). It matches/exceeds reported AlphaEvolve results on 5/7 tasks, but AlphaEvolve’s iteration budget is unavailable, so cost parity is unestablished (§6.1). On six systems tasks it has the best mean on all six GPT-5 tasks and 5/6 Gemini tasks (Table 3, §6.2, p. 12). Frontier-CS’s 172-problem aggregate is mean 62.6, median 75.5, versus OpenEvolve 50.5/56.2 (Figure 3, §6.3, p. 13). The Heilbronn cost figure reports under-$1 generation cost for EvoX and GEPA to exceed 0.031, versus $7.6 ShinkaEvolve and $15.4 OpenEvolve; this is one task and LLM-generation cost only (Figure 4b, §6.4, p. 14). Appendix Table 7 reports ARC-AGI final accuracy 48% vs 41% (GPT-5) and 51% vs 45% (Gemini), with 30 iterations, but the authors caution that ARC’s strict train/test setup differs from adaptive optimization (Appendix B.1, p. 24).

**Limitations and appraisal.** The comparison is strong for breadth and repeated math/system runs, but Frontier-CS/ALE aggregation has no reported run intervals and several prior baselines use results from other papers or unavailable budgets. Three runs are task/backbone runs, not independent task populations; the method still depends on a fixed evaluator, generator model, 100-iteration envelope, and human-designed validity/stagnation rules. The cost result omits evaluator and strategy-generation accounting beyond the plotted LLM generation measure. The qualitative search-evolution cases are illustrative. These are evidence limitations and analyst interpretation; the paper does not claim independent replication.

**Rubric and disposition.** **R H:** direct meta-improvement of the evolution process and broad overlap with the session’s central question. **E M:** substantial breadth plus three-run math/system tables, but mixed baseline provenance, incomplete cost accounting, and weak uncertainty for the 182 algorithmic tasks. **N H:** explicitly co-evolves search strategy with solutions; distinct from Hyperagents’ editable improver and Dream-RSI’s replay-based controller refinement. **T H:** clear two-level loop, stagnation trigger, and useful cost/transfer discussion. **C M:** important omission now resolved, but overlaps Hyperagents/GEPA/Dream-RSI’s meta-search slot and does not add weight adaptation or persistent cross-task state. **Decision: reserve, first alternate if the session shifts toward meta-search mechanics; retain the current ten because Hyperagents supplies the selected meta-improvement anchor and R-Zero/SEAL/AgentStream broaden mechanisms and controls.**

## MLEvolve: A Self-Evolving Framework for Automated Machine Learning Algorithm Discovery

**Metadata and access.** Shangheng Du, Xiangchao Yan, Jinxin Shi, Zongsheng Cao, Shiyang Feng, Zichen Liang, Boyuan Sun, Tianshuo Peng, Yifan Zhou, Xin Li, Jie Zhou, Liang He, Bo Zhang, and Lei Bai (Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory; East China Normal University). [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06473), [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06473v1), [v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06473v1). Submitted 2026-06-04; v1 is the latest version available at the cutoff. ArXiv grants only its perpetual non-exclusive distribution license ([license](https://arxiv.org/licenses/nonexclusive-distrib/1.0/)); no paper copy is retained. The [public implementation](https://github.com/InternScience/MLEvolve) is Apache-2.0 (repository LICENSE/README). Download URL for coordinator: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06473v1`.

**Question and method (~200 words).** MLEvolve targets long-horizon end-to-end ML engineering, where branches otherwise isolate information and repeated planning is memoryless. Progressive MCGS extends MCTS into a graph: primary edges preserve parent-child credit assignment while reference edges carry information across branches. UCT exploration is progressively mixed with elite-guided exploitation, with branch/global stagnation triggers for intra-branch evolution, cross-branch reference, or multi-branch aggregation (§3.2, pp. 4–7). Retrospective Memory combines a curated task-type knowledge base with dynamic global records of plans, outcomes, analyses, and feedback; lexical and FAISS retrieval are fused by reciprocal rank fusion (§3.3, pp. 7–8). Hierarchical planning separates module-level “what/why” from code “how,” choosing base, stepwise, or diff generation modes (§3.4, pp. 8–9). The primary benchmark is MLE-Bench’s 75 Kaggle tasks (22 low, 38 medium, 15 high); a second evaluation uses 15 AlphaEvolve mathematical optimization tasks (§4.1, p. 9). MLEvolve uses Gemini-3.1-Pro-preview, temperature 1, at most 500 expansions and 12 hours per task on 21 vCPUs, 234 GB RAM, and one H200. MLE-Bench Table 1 reports mean ± SEM over three seeds; ablations use 22 tasks and a 9-task mechanism subset without reported intervals.

**Key evidence and locators.** On all 75 MLE-Bench tasks, MLEvolve reports medal rate 65.3±0.8%, low/medium/high rates 80.3±1.5/64.0±0.9/46.7±0.0, valid submissions 100.0±0.0%, above-median 76.0±2.3%, and gold 34.7±0.0%, over three seeds (Table 1, §4.2, pp. 9–10). Compared with heterogeneous leaderboard/paper baselines, it uses half the standard 24-hour budget; this is not a fully budget- or backbone-matched causal comparison. On the 15 math tasks, it is the best reported method on 11/15 and matches/surpasses AlphaEvolve on 14/15 (Table 2, p. 10; exact task values in Table 2). On 22 MLE-Bench Lite tasks, removing Progressive MCGS changes medal 81.82→68.18%, removing Retrospective Memory 81.82→68.18%, and removing adaptive code generation 81.82→72.73%; beat ratio falls 88.39→79.91/81.90/84.14 (Table 3, p. 10). On the 9-task mechanism subset, removing intra-branch evolution lowers medal 66.67→33.33% (Table 5, Appendix E, p. 18). The reported outcomes are task-level percentages; no dollar/token ledger is given.

**Limitations and appraisal.** The strongest positive result has three seeds, but baseline rows come from different models, times, and sources, so “state of the art” is not a clean matched control. MLE-Bench’s 75 tasks are benchmark instances, not independent deployments; the 22- and 9-task ablations are small and lack uncertainty. The 15 math comparisons have no repeated-run dispersion and reuse a specialized prior task suite. The dynamic memory includes a hand-curated cold-start knowledge base, and the evaluator/benchmark remains externally supplied. These limits narrow claims about general self-evolution or recursive improvement; they do not negate the measured MLE-Bench result.

**Rubric and disposition.** **R H:** directly tests experience reuse, adaptive search, and long-horizon agent improvement. **E M:** three-seed full benchmark and component ablations are useful, while controls, uncertainty, and baseline provenance are limited. **N H:** graph-based cross-branch memory plus progressive MCGS and adaptive coding is a distinct mechanism family. **T H:** concrete architecture and ablations make branch sharing, memory, and planner/coder separation teachable. **C M:** adds a broad ML-engineering domain and open implementation, but overlaps FinEvo-Bench’s professional workflow and AgentStream’s state/stream coverage; it does not displace the retained mechanisms. **Decision: reserve, with higher priority than EvoX if a practical ML-engineering section is added; retain the ten because FinEvo-Bench already covers professional longitudinal evaluation and the selected set has stronger complementary counterfactual, weight-adaptation, and meta-improvement anchors.**
